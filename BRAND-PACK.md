A bookmark **rescue** tool for people with thousands of saved links they've stopped being able to use. It scans an existing library, shows the user exactly what's in it and what's wrong with it, makes it searchable, and helps them clean it up safely.

## 7. Core Messaging Pillars

In priority order. The first two are the differentiated ones; the rest support them.

### A. Revelation ⭐
*Show people the truth about their own library.* Nobody has ever told them they have 47 duplicates and 1,104 pages untouched for two years. This is the hero moment and the thing no competitor does.

### B. Rescue ⭐
*Fix it in one sitting, not one bookmark at a time.* The job is finite and satisfying — and it is the job every competitor skipped.

### C. Retrieval
*Find any saved page in seconds.* The acute, in-the-moment pain that makes someone install something today.

### D. Safety
*Nothing is deleted without you, and everything can be undone.* Not fine print — a headline claim, because we are asking to touch a decade of someone's saved things.

### E. Trust
*Your bookmarks never leave your machine.* Literally true for most users via on-device AI. Reinforces D.

### Retired
- ~~Organization~~ — subsumed into Rescue, and it was the crowded, chronic-pain framing
- ~~Utility ("turn bookmarks into lists")~~ — a Phase 2 feature, not a brand pillar

## 10. Longer Product Description

Salvage is a bookmark management extension for Chrome and Edge that automatically sorts saved links into useful categories, adds tags and summaries, detects duplicates and broken links, and makes everything searchable. It turns bookmarks from a messy archive into a personal internet library.

|---|---|

| Bookmark cleanup tools built in | ⚠️ Partly — Raindrop has a duplicate finder, but **retroactive cleanup of an existing messy library is genuinely under-served**. This is the real opening. |

### The differentiators worth leading with

1. **"Raindrop wants you to start over. We fix the mess you already have."** Every competitor optimizes the *save* flow; none of them do retroactive cleanup of a decade-old bookmark tree well. That is the specific, under-served pain.
2. **Genuinely local-first** — with on-device AI, page content never leaves the machine. Trust reinforcement for (1), not the headline.

Everything else on the original list is a feature, not a differentiator, and should not carry marketing weight.

## 12A. The decision — Salvage

### Why Salvage

**It is the only candidate that passed both checks, twice.** That sentence is the whole argument, and it is worth more than a better metaphor that has not been verified — which is precisely what Cairn was.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Chrome Web Store exact-name match | ✅ None. Verified 2026-08-19 and again 2026-08-20 |
| Chrome Web Store category overlap | ✅ **Zero.** Every hit is auto-auction, crypto, gaming, or repair estimation — a completely different semantic territory |
| General software landscape | ✅ No software product named "Salvage". SalvageData Recovery is a distinct mark in a distinct service |
| Fits the settled positioning | ✅ Rescue, literally |
| Not a `*mark`/`*link` compound | ✅ |
| Not AI-buzzwordy | ✅ Ages well |

The semantic distance is real protection, not just an absence of collisions. Nobody searching "salvage" in an extension store is looking for a bookmark tool, and nobody looking for a bookmark tool will land on a salvage-yard product. That is a cleaner separation than a crowded-but-adjacent name would give.

### The honest caveat, and how the copy handles it

"Salvage" carries wreckage connotations — *salvage yard*, *salvage title*. This product must never imply that someone's saved things are junk ([MVP.md](MVP.md), "never shame the user").

**The resolution is that salvage means recovering what is valuable from something damaged.** The wreck is the *filing*, never the bookmarks. The copy must lean on that deliberately and consistently:

- ✅ "There's a lot worth keeping in here." · "Recover what you saved." · "Nine years of saved things, finally findable."
- ❌ "Clean up your mess." · "Rescue your junk." · Anything positioning the library as wreckage.

If launch copy ever reads as though the user's library is the wreck, the name is being used wrongly — fix the copy, not the name.

### Store listing title — a separate string from the product name

Per [PRD.md](PRD.md) §24, the listing title carries the keywords:

> **Salvage — Clean Up Duplicate & Broken Bookmarks**

`extensionName` is deliberately kept to the short form **"Salvage"**, because it is interpolated into report copy (*"Salvage found your library: 3,142 bookmarks…"*) where the long title would read badly. **A separate short-name/title split is still needed** before packaging so the manifest can carry the keyword title without breaking in-UI copy — flagged as build work, not a branding decision.

### Rejected at the final step

- **Rummage** — the warmest fit and zero store results, but US trademark **86163193** (filed 2014) covers *"software for the collection, editing, organizing, modifying"* in class 9. Its live/dead status could not be resolved from public search. **Not rejected on merit — rejected because it could not be verified.** If formal clearance later shows that mark is dead, Rummage is a legitimate reopen.
- **Reckon** — zero store results, but Reckon Limited is an ASX-listed accounting software company (RKN, founded 1987, AU/NZ/US). Dead.
- **Muster** — store-clear, but thinly verified generally, and "Muster AI" already exists in-store.

### ~~✅ Recommendation: **Cairn**~~ — SUPERSEDED, see the retraction below

*/kɛərn/ — a stack of stones left on a trail so you can find your way back.*

**Why this name and not another.** A cairn is a marker you leave for your future self. That is precisely what a bookmark is, and precisely what the product is about — not saving things, but *finding your way back to what you saved*. The metaphor also carries the mess: cairns accumulate, one stone at a time, over years, until the trail is crowded with them. And it carries the rescue: a cairn is what you look for when you are lost.

The test of a good product name is whether the tagline writes itself. This one does:

> **Cairn — find your way back to what you saved.**

Further reasons it fits this specific product:

| Criterion | Cairn |
|---|---|
| Short, spellable, one word | ✅ 5 letters |
| Metaphor pays off in the tagline | ✅ Rare, and the strongest signal |
| Not AI-buzzwordy | ✅ Ages well; "AI" in a name dates fast |
| Not another `*mark` / `*link` compound | ✅ Escapes the crowded, forgettable naming pool the earlier shortlist sat in |
| Clear of the affiliate/marketing space | ✅ The exact trap Linkwise fell into |
| Fits a rescue tool, not a save tool | ✅ Matches the settled positioning |

### What I verified — and what I did not

**Checked (web search, 2026-08-16):** no bookmark manager, browser extension, or consumer productivity product named Cairn. Existing software uses are in unrelated B2B categories — an internal-apps platform (`getcairns.dev`), a help-desk reseller (Cairn Software), and a dev-services firm (Cairnstack). Different markets, different trademark classes, no consumer-facing collision. This is a *far* weaker conflict than Linkwise's head-on collision with an affiliate network.

### ❌ Cairn is DEAD — verified 2026-08-19

> **Retraction.** An earlier 2026-08-19 edit to this section stated that a Chrome Web Store check had been done and that Cairn was "clear." **That was wrong and has been removed.** The check used a domain-filtered web search, which queries the web index restricted to a host — it does **not** query the store's own search. It returned generic category pages, and a null result was written up as a verified pass. The error was caught by the project owner, who found a colliding listing by hand within minutes.
>
> **Method rule, now mandatory for any name check:** query the store's search endpoint directly — `https://chromewebstore.google.com/search/<term>` — and validate the method against a known-positive term before trusting a negative result. **A null result from a search engine is not evidence of absence.**

**The Chrome Web Store contains at least nine "Cairn" extensions**, two of which are direct competitors in this exact product space:

| Listing | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Cairn** *(exact name match)* | *"Your second brain for the web — save, organize, and rediscover"* — 5.0★. **This is our product description almost verbatim, including "rediscover."** |
| **Cairn: AI Second Brain — Save Pages, PDFs & YouTube, Then Ask** | c-ai.dev. Saves pages, auto-tags, answers natural-language questions over your library. 4 users, updated 2 Aug 2026. Clusters saves into **"trails."** |
| Cairn — Mark your path on LinkedIn | Also uses the trail metaphor |
| Cairn Wallet · Cairn — YouTube Course Tracker · Cairn — Claude Export · Cairn · Importeur d'offres | Unrelated categories |

Plus **Cairn Tasks** (Apple App Store, 2026, Dacite Dev LLC) — a local-first, no-account to-do app, i.e. our privacy positioning in a neighbouring category.

**Why this is disqualifying, beyond the raw collision count:**

The entire justification for Cairn above is the trail-marker metaphor. **Two shipped extensions already use "trail" as a product concept**, and one describes itself in our exact words. The metaphor is not distinctive — it is the *obvious* metaphor for this product, which is precisely why the space is crowded. A name whose whole argument is a metaphor that competitors have already shipped has no argument left.

**Do not use Cairn. This is closed.**

### ⚠️ The strategic point this whole exercise surfaced

**The name matters less than this document assumes, and the effort is being spent on the wrong dimension.**

Chrome Web Store discovery is keyword-driven ([PRD.md](PRD.md) §24). The listing title that wins is shaped like:

> **`<Name>` — Clean Up Duplicate & Broken Bookmarks**

The *keywords* do the acquisition work. The name only has to be **clear, unclaimed, and not embarrassing** — it does not have to be clever. Every evocative metaphor name tried so far (Cairn, Waypoint) turned out to be crowded *because* it was the obvious metaphor, and each one cost a research cycle to discover that.

**Recommendation: stop optimising for metaphor.** Pick a plain, unclaimed, low-collision name, verify it with the store-search method above plus a class 9 trademark search, and spend the saved effort on the listing keywords, which is where installs actually come from.

### ⚠️ Why "Linkwise" must be retired

**"Linkwise" is an existing affiliate marketing network.** Launched in 2008, it is the largest affiliate network in Greece and describes itself as the leading affiliate network in South-East Europe. It operates at **linkwi.se**, is a business unit of ATCOM SA, and built its own affiliate platform in 2012.

This is a particularly bad collision for this specific product, because the plan's second revenue stream is **affiliate marketing**. We would be an affiliate-monetized product sharing a name with an established affiliate network — in the same industry, competing for the same search terms, and creating exactly the kind of confusion that draws a trademark complaint rather than a polite email.

Beyond the legal exposure, the name has a second problem: **"Linkwise" describes a bookmark manager, and we are not building a bookmark manager.** It says nothing about rescue, retrieval, or fixing an existing mess — the whole basis of the strategy. Even without the conflict, it would be the wrong name for the product now being built.

`linkwise.com` and `linkwi.se` are both in use. A rename after launch costs the store listing, accumulated reviews, SEO, and every backlink — so this is a decision to make now, once, and not revisit.

### 🚨 Worse than recorded — verified 2026-08-19

The 2026-08-16 entry treated this as a trademark conflict in the abstract. It is more concrete than that:

> **"Linkwise for Chrome" is a live Chrome Web Store extension, rated 4.9★, published by the Linkwise affiliate network.** It shows affiliate program details and generates affiliate deeplinks with SubIDs.

This means the collision is **inside the exact storefront we would publish to**, not merely in a trademark register:

- Two extensions named Linkwise in one store is a brand-confusion and listing-rejection risk, not a theoretical one
- Searching "Linkwise" in the Chrome Web Store surfaces *their* affiliate tool — so the name actively sends our traffic to someone else
- Our own Phase 3 plan involves affiliate monetisation. Publishing an affiliate-capable extension, in the same store, under the name of an established affiliate network's own extension, is close to the worst available combination

**Linkwise is not merely inadvisable. It is unusable.** Treat this as closed.

## 16. Launch Positioning

**Lead with the rescue, not the management.** The store listing, the demo video, and the first Reddit post should all show the same thing: a real, ugly, 3,000-bookmark library, and the report that reveals what is wrong with it.

### Best launch message

> **See what's actually in your bookmarks — then fix it.**
> Scan your library in about a minute. Find the duplicates, the dead links, and the 1,100 pages you haven't opened in two years. Clean it up safely, with undo on everything.

### The demo asset

One 20-second video, no narration:
1. Click "Scan My Bookmarks"
2. Progress bar over a genuinely messy real library
3. The Library Report appears — the numbers are the punchline
4. One click: 47 duplicates previewed and removed
5. One search: a page found instantly that was buried nine folders deep

This is the whole marketing plan for launch. It is shareable because the numbers are *someone's real mess*, and everyone watching recognizes their own.

### What not to say at launch

- Do not mention affiliate or discovery modules. They are not built, they are policy-constrained, and mentioning revenue intent at launch costs trust for a tool that reads all your bookmarks ([MONETIZATION-PLAN.md](MONETIZATION-PLAN.md) §12).
- Do not promise summaries or smart lists until they ship in Phase 2 ([ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md)). *(Broken-link detection moved into the MVP on 2026-08-19 and may be promised at launch.)*
- Do not lead with "AI". It is how the product works, not why anyone wants it — and it is what every competitor's listing already says.
